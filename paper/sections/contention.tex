\section{A secondary finding: GPU contention}
\label{sec:contention}

We also ran a co-located synthetic GPU job alongside the prefill-heavy workload, with a
sham condition and a recovery baseline. The job was busy for 25\% or 50\% of each
100\,ms period, its kernels ran 14\% and 28\% of the time, and the two processes
shared the GPU by time-slicing. At the 50\% setting, the job
cost 13--15\% in end-to-end latency and slowed requests even at C1, where it slowed
prefill more than decode (+22.5\% against +11.3\%). We have no tested explanation for
that difference; a post hoc check found the GPU clock 3--4\% lower under contention at
C1 (median 1680 against 1740\,MHz), which may account for part of the slowdown. Mean
\texttt{nvidia-smi} utilization moved only 1--6 points, against a 3.5-point spread
between identical control runs; it was already about 90\% at C1 without
contention, which left little room to move, so it is a poor detector of this
interference \citep{nvidiasmi}.

Of seven predictions, four held, two were mixed, and one failed. Utilization stayed
within 5 points at C1 but moved 6.0 points at C8. Queue time at C8 rose only 6\%, less
than prefill (+17\%), against the prediction, and the share of latency before the
first token did not rise, the one outright failure. We observed this contrast with
added load only under our closed-loop client.
