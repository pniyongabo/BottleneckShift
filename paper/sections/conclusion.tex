\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On this setup, from C16 to C32 at the default budget, the added delay under load went
into per-step time while steps filled toward the budget's cap. With the smallest
budget it went into the queue, and past the cap it must. A prompt chunk's cost grows
with the context it attends over, in line with a count of attention FLOPs. Whether a
step's cost tracks its scheduled tokens under load remains open: the pre-registered
test was rejected at C8, and its pass at C32 used a lagged measure. The token budget
decides how prompt work is cut into steps, and so trades median against tail latency.
Concurrency decides how many steps carry prompt work.

Four directions follow:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{A step-cost model:} cost as a function of scheduled tokens, context,
    and KV-cache reads, in the style of Vidur \citep{agrawal2024vidur}, checked
    against execution time at C32 and tested by whether it predicts ITL p50 and p99
    across concurrency and budgets.
  \item \emph{Open-loop load with varied lengths:} to see whether the per-step account
    and packing hold when queues can grow and requests do not arrive in groups.
  \item \emph{Larger models and multi-GPU serving,} where communication adds a third
    component to step cost.
  \item \emph{Measurement-driven backend selection,} which we deferred: our
    single-server data cannot evaluate a selection policy without invented rewards,
    so it needs a new two-backend experiment.
\end{itemize}
