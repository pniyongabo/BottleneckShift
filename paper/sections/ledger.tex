\paragraph{The hypothesis was wrong.}
Prefill did not dominate the prefill-heavy workload, time before the first token did
not dominate past saturation (Section~\ref{sec:load}), and short prompts were packed
instead of making steps smaller (Section~\ref{sec:stepcost}).

\paragraph{The instrument could not see the effect.}
The iteration-token histogram could not show per-step chunk sizes, so Phase D's
manipulation check failed as specified; that miss led to the step log, whose interval
then turned out to lag by one step. \texttt{nvidia-smi} utilization is a busy fraction
averaged over the driver's own sample period \citep{nvidiasmi}, so no polling rate
could resolve 40\,ms prefills.

\paragraph{The scheduler and the load generator shaped the result.}
Queue growth past saturation was sub-linear (not confirmed), and contention raised
queue time less than prefill time (mixed). Both fit a scheduler that serves running
requests first and turns waiting into longer steps, and a closed-loop client that caps
how many requests can wait.

\paragraph{A test was too strict for its data.}
The rank statistic of step size and interval cleared its threshold at C32 but not at
C8. Forward and reverse condition orders differed by 0.1--2.5\% in latency and
throughput, enough to fail a strict range-overlap test in Phase B. With three runs per
order, two ranges fail to overlap by chance one time in ten for each metric, so across
about ten metrics a few failures were expected.

Two central results, the per-step crossover and the context measurements, were tested
directly only because an earlier prediction failed or prompted a post hoc reading.
