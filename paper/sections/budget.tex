\section{The token budget trades median against tail}
\label{sec:budget}

If the jump in per-step time comes from prompt-carrying steps, the token budget
(Section~\ref{sec:background}) should control it. Its default, 2048, equals the prompt
length above.

At C32 we ran budgets of 512, 2048, and 8192 (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). A 512-token
budget spreads prompt work over many small steps: ITL is nearly flat (p50 15.8\ms,
p99 17.8\ms), but TTFT triples, throughput falls 10\%, and queueing takes two thirds
of each request's latency (1.2 of 1.8\,s). At 2048, most steps carry a full 2048-token
chunk (in Milestone 5's step log, 77\% of steps carried prompt work), so p50 and p99
are both near 55--59\ms. At 8192, several prompts share fewer, larger steps: the
median falls to 6.9\ms{} and throughput rises 18\%, but p99 rises to 169\ms. The
budget with the best median has the worst tail. Against the documentation's guidance,
8192 also raised mean TTFT relative to 2048; without each run's start-up burst, mean TTFT
was 1{,}542, 217, and 609\ms{} for 512, 2048, and 8192 (post hoc). By mean TPOT, the order is 512
(15\ms), 8192 (26\ms), and 2048 (45\ms).

We had predicted that ITL p50 and p99 would both order 512 $<$ 2048 $<$ 8192. The
p99 ordering held and the p50 ordering did not. The manipulation check relied on the
iteration-token histogram and failed as specified, so under the pre-registered rule the
test of the mechanism was inconclusive. If we accept server-log evidence that the
budgets were applied, a deviation from the protocol, the pre-registered form of the
step-size explanation (a p50 that rises with the budget) is unsupported. The open
mechanism motivated Milestone 5.

\paragraph{Prompt length against budget.}
Milestone 5 varied the prompt length $L \in \{1024, 2048, 4096\}$ at budgets 2048 and
8192 with the step log on (Table~\ref{tab:lengths}). At C32
and $B{=}2048$, ITL p99 was 58\ms{} for 2048-token prompts and 65\ms{} for 4096-token
prompts: past the budget, prompts are cut into budget-sized chunks, so the tail grew
only 11\%. At $B{=}8192$ the tail was about 3$\times$ higher (170 and 200\ms), and the
median stayed below 10\ms. We predicted that 1024-token prompts would have a tail at
least 25\% lower than 2048-token prompts. It was 19\% lower, because the scheduler
packed two 1024-token prompts into one 2048-token step (post hoc;
Section~\ref{sec:stepcost}). At C8 and $B{=}2048$, ITL p50 was 4.6--5.7\ms{} for every
prompt length, as predicted, against 55--57\ms{} at C32 for $L \ge 2048$. The tail fell
less, to 40--54\ms, and packing again limited the $L{=}1024$ tail (11\% lower, against
a predicted 25\%). At C8 with $B{=}8192$, prompt length did move the tail: p99 rose
from 60 to 178\ms{} between $L{=}1024$ and $L{=}4096$.
